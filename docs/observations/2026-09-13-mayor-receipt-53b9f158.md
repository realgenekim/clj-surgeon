## 4. Why REFUSE, and what it does NOT mean

The skiff could not satisfy the gate's admission floor. It needs **3584 MiB** of
declared headroom; the box offered **3271-3378 MiB** while running at load **63-90**.

I refused to force it. `GATE_MEMAVAIL_MIB` declares *what the box may lend* and is
explicitly unvalidated operator authority — a receipt resting on a headroom figure I
invented would be precisely the self-certification this exercise exists to eliminate.
Likewise I did not set `TMPDIR` or any other flag: the point of an independent receipt
is to run the thing as shipped.

I also tried to earn the headroom honestly rather than declare it: reaped my own
order-hub nREPL, and waited in a poll loop until free memory cleared the floor
(3866 → 3888 MiB) before launching. **It still refused, reading 3271 MiB.**

**Observation, offered as a hypothesis and NOT as a finding:** across both runs the
gate's own reading came in **507-617 MiB below** what `vm_stat` reported seconds
earlier, immediately before launch. If the gate measures available memory *after* its
own JVM and classpath are resident, then on a box near the floor the act of measuring
costs enough memory to guarantee the refusal. I did not instrument this and cannot
distinguish it from ordinary volatility on a load-63 machine. **If it is real, the
admission check is not reliably satisfiable near the floor, which is worth a look.**
