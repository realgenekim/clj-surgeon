## THE DEFECT IS FIXED, AND THAT IS THE RESULT THAT MATTERS

The reason arm E2 failed its gate is gone.

* **Receipt content.** The E2 phantom deletion (`[1822 nil "    ;; every live legacy key has
  been rotated…" nil]`) does not appear in any E3 receipt: `grep -c` = **0**. The whole
  `:comment_edits` block is now one `{:deleted "    (print (calendar-ics e))))" :line 1241}`,
  one `:changed` paren rebalance, and six `:moved` owner entries — against E2's 40 positional
  rows, ~30 of which paired untouched comments with their neighbours.
* **Caller verdict, post-hoc rule `defect-rule.py`, one rule over both arms:** arms whose
  final report states the comment fact is wrong — **E2: 6/6. E3: 1/6**, and reading that one
  hit (Y6) shows it is a hypothetical about what an identity diff *could* be wrong about, not
  a claim that this one was. Substantively **0/6**.
* Every E3 arm used `:comment_edits` as its account of the trap resolution. Y2 called it "the
  most useful single item"; Y1 said it proved the fixed decision "at line granularity rather
  than inferred from a diff"; Y5 said it made the paren accounting visible.

**So why does the comment-fallback clause still read 6/6?** Because the preregistered REPORT
rule asks whether a report line names comments and a by-hand read in the same breath — and in
E3 the sentences that trip it are *praise*. Two of the six matched lines are Y1's and Y2's
descriptions of what the fact saved them from doing. The rule was validated against E2, where
"comments" and "git diff" co-occurred only in complaints; on a build where the fact works, the
same words appear in the opposite sentiment and the rule cannot tell them apart. **That is a
defect in my own preregistered instrument, disclosed here rather than after the numbers, and
the gate is reported FAILED on the rule as written.** The corrected, post-hoc reading —
0/6 arms found the fact wrong, 5/6 still read the named block once to confirm paren balance
