## ONE LEARNING

**Moving the facts into the receipt fixed adoption completely and fixed inspection not at all
— because callers were never inspecting to discover; they were inspecting to falsify.**

Stage 3 put the same facts behind a separate read-only `:facts-only` projection and **0 of 6**
callers used it inside the clock. Trunk `c5c89be5` emits them inside the receipt the caller
already has, and **6 of 6** read them, unprompted beyond one paragraph of brief. All six named
the same prize: the per-file caller-site table the brief deliberately withholds (9 files,
43 sites), handed over already reconciled. That is a complete reversal of the stage-3 zero, and
it says the barrier was never the value of the facts — it was the round trip.

And inspection barely moved: paired median ratio **0.98**, paired median delta **−0.5 actions**.
The unpaired medians (12 → 9) and the cohort totals (65 → 53) show a real but modest fall, and
the paired figure is the honest one.

The six explanations agree, unprompted, and they are specific. `:facts` is a record of **what
the transaction wrote**. Every check the callers still ran is a **negative** or an **absence**:

* the `(comment …)` policy edit — the task's stated architectural trap — has **no fact at all**;
  `:comment_policy` is echoed from the request and nothing says what was removed. Every arm
  read that diff by hand; four independently proposed the same key,
  `:facts :comment_edits [{:file … :line … :removed …}]`;
* `:facts` never says "no stale reference remains", "no facade was emitted", "each owner is
  defined exactly once repo-wide", or "bodies are byte-preserved" — which are precisely the
  oracle's checks, and precisely what the callers re-derived. E6 put the principle plainly:
  *a receipt counting its own writes cannot witness a site it never saw*;
* the destination `ns` form is absent, so three arms opened the file to confirm the dependency
  direction and which alias won.

**So the next move for this row is not more emission of what happened. It is emission of what
is now true** — the negative statements — or an explicit refusal to claim them, so a caller can
tell the difference between "verified absent" and "not asserted."

## THE TWO NEW READS WENT UNUSED, 0/6 AND 0/6 — AND THAT IS AN ORDERING FACT, NOT A DEFECT

Both stage-3 product findings are genuinely fixed, and the runner verified both before the
cohort ran:

* **NS-SPLIT-053** — a post-commit `:facts-only` read now answers from the committed snapshot
  in **0.76 s** (`:facts_basis "committed-transaction"`). Stage 3's finding #1 was that the
  projection refused at the one moment a caller reached for it. Fixed.
* **NS-SPLIT-054** — a pre-commit facts read carries a `:manifest` that is a complete request
  with `:verification {:profile …}` and `:plan_only true`. Stage 3's finding #2 was that the
  printed manifest was not a valid request. Fixed.

Neither was called once. Five of six wrote out why, and the argument is the same twice:

* read (a) is **dominated by NS-SPLIT-052** — the committed receipt already carries the
  identical `:facts` inline, so the sub-second re-read returns bytes the caller has open;
* read (b) costs ~40 s to plan a request that was frozen, runner-prepared and unmodifiable.
  E4: *"read (b) earns its 40 s only when you distrust the manifest before committing."*
  E6: *"A plan is a weaker witness than a receipt of the finished transaction."*

When the split is the caller's first call and it succeeds, the pre-commit read never happens
and the post-commit read is redundant. The named remaining uses are real but narrow: a session
that lost its receipt, a `:facts`/tree disagreement, or a caller who wants to inspect the plan
before committing.

## TWO FINDINGS THAT ARE NOT ABOUT THE FACTS

**`verification_complete false` is a live foot-gun, named independently by all six.** With the
runner's no-op profile it is *always* false on a perfectly committed, correct transaction. The
brief warned them; every arm observed that a caller applying the standing proof rule
mechanically would read a good split as unproven, and could re-run a completed transaction.
`:proof_pending ["cold-suite"]` is the field doing the honest work. E3 proposed a tri-state
(`:not-attempted`) instead of a bare `false`.

**An arithmetic error in the retained stage-2 control.** `results-stage2.edn` records
`:authorship {:vals-s [97.7 126.6 143.5 111.7 140.0 153.5] :median-s 126.6}`. The median of
those six is **133.3**; 126.6 is the third-smallest. Stage 3's `compute-stage3.py` used 133.3,
so the error is confined to that one field. Clause (c) was preregistered against the erroneous
126.6 and **passes on both numbers** (121.5 ≤ 126.6 and ≤ 133.3), so the verdict is unaffected.
Recorded because a wrong median in a retained control file will be read again.

## ONE CAVEAT

**The caller family deviates from the instruction, deliberately, and the cohort is bounded by
its brief.** The coordinator named six Sol callers; the retained D control is Opus
(`freeze-stage2.edn`: `:model "opus, one fresh caller per arm"`), as are the stage-1 N floor
controls. Running Sol against an Opus control would confound every clause of the gate with
caller family, so **E ran Opus, matched to D**. A Sol replication is a separate cohort needing
its own Sol native and Sol D controls, and nothing here transfers to it.

The second half of the caveat is the one stage 3 also carried: **this brief hands every arm the
25-form list with line numbers, the 47-edge dependency graph and the retained-var table.** The
genuine marginal content of `:facts` over that is the caller-site table and the promotion
counts. A thinner brief would very likely show a larger inspection reduction — and a repository
whose split is not preceded by a hand-written manifest is the case the fleet actually cares
about. **The 0.98 is a 0.98 at this task.**

## WHAT'S NEXT

1. **Emit the absences.** `:facts :comment_edits`, and an explicit statement of the four
   negatives the oracle checks (no stale reference, no facade, exactly-once repo-wide, bodies
   byte-preserved) — or a typed refusal to claim them. This is the change the cohort's own
   callers designed, four of them in nearly the same words.
2. **Re-run the inspection meter against a thinner brief.** The caller-site table is the only
   marginal content `:facts` adds over this brief. On a task where the form list and dependency
   graph are *not* supplied, the inspection reduction should be much larger — and that is the
   case a real caller is in.
3. **Fix `:median-s` in `results-stage2.edn`** and consider whether `verification_complete`
   should become a tri-state.
4. **Do not spend another cohort on the separate reads.** NS-SPLIT-053 and NS-SPLIT-054 both
   work and both are structurally unreachable on the happy path of this route. Their value is in
   the recovery and pre-commit-review paths, which is where they should next be measured.
