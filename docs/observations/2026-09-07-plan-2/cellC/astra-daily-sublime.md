**1. Attack Fable’s loop, then commit.**

Its strongest feature is the transfer: a wart encountered during useful work becomes a witness in the tool’s own repair loop, then a protection for the next caller. Reviewers contribute executable counterexamples to the same corpus. The tree exposes what has earned admission. Delivery checks close the gap between a local repair and another seat actually benefiting. This is substantially better than a separate “improve developer experience” project that never meets a user.

But “every refusal → round” would build a factory for expanding the apparatus. A correct refusal is sometimes the product working. Capture all attempts mechanically; distinguish expected protection, caller repair, false refusal, unavailable capability, and unknown execution. Escalate the *hand-paid obstruction*, including native workarounds with no tool refusal. Deduplicate a recurring class while retaining every occurrence and its cost. A finding needs a trigger and owner; it does not automatically deserve implementation.

The findings file should be a generated, frozen selection of existing friction/inbox IDs, with evidence, violated intent, and acceptance. The builder should not receive a second handwritten brief. Preserve nuance as a short judgment field when needed. The tree is the shared ordering and phase view; the inbox owns actionable findings; neither needs another manually synchronized queue.

“One landing per day” should mean a daily opportunity to land a ready batch. A zero-change day can be excellent. A daily quota rewards splitting fixes, hiding pending proof, and manufacturing work. Review and the appropriate battery gate each final landing candidate once; they need not tax every inner iteration. Existing required intent reviews still apply, and a timed-out review remains pending. Measurement may start on an oracle-clean, qualified candidate without waiting for landing review. Landing goes to the authorized working branch, currently `MCP/main`; public `main` remains frozen.

Also, the named entrances are not interchangeable or universally shipped. `make test-probe` is explicitly a proposed contract unless the repository implements it. `round` and `papercut-oracle` are program-specific. Neither should be pointed at arbitrary repositories as fleet infrastructure. Native editing remains the ordinary route, with automatic Surgeon use limited to the admitted witnessed contracts. Making every seat invoke more tools would defeat the objective.

My commitment: **capture by default, repair by evidence, deliver by contract, promote by independent proof.** Keep the existing entrances and extend only the seam a real next task needs.

**2. What actually recurses, and where it must stop.**

Repeated activity is another warm probe, another daily report, or another weekly pair under unchanged rules. Improvement is a defect getting fixed. Recursion occurs when the defect is in the machinery that captures, diagnoses, repairs, verifies, or distributes those improvements—and the repaired machinery makes the *next improvement* cheaper or more trustworthy.

For example: a caller loses time interpreting a refusal; its trace becomes a red witness; the operation gains an actionable next call; a later fresh caller completes without that repair. Or a supervisor waits for the wrong child; its actual trace becomes a lifecycle witness; the corrected supervisor shortens every subsequent repair batch. Or an observer misses hidden execution; a frozen negative exposes that blind spot; the observer correction prevents later false certification. These are three distinct benefits: less work, shorter delay, and fewer wrong beliefs. Record which was earned.

The recursive claim needs a second encounter. “We built a helper” proves construction. “A later eligible task used it and avoided the original obstruction, with equal acceptance” proves transfer. Prefer a different seat or repository for that witness when the claimed scope crosses them. Retain lack of opportunity as untested transfer, not success. A ratchet also has maintenance cost; remove redundant machinery when its promise remains enforced by a simpler mechanism. Otherwise recursion grows the tax it was supposed to remove.

The fixed point is **authority over intent and evidence**, not an eternally frozen implementation. During a measurement epoch, freeze the task manifest, acceptance meaning, oracle and harness versions, effective prompt, native control, resource policy, endpoints, and denominator. A candidate may improve the runner; it cannot certify its improvement by replacing the running grader midway.

Use this change rule:

- The builder may propose block, oracle, or harness changes in an isolated candidate, retaining the triggering evidence. The currently trusted package remains the reference.
- A named independent instrument steward verifies the change against frozen positive and negative cases at the production entrance. Different role and independent evidence matter more than merely changing model names. Where an oracle missed a bug, repair it with the fix, as doctrine requires, but qualify that oracle repair separately.
- Behavioral implementation repairs that preserve approved meaning may land through existing review authority. Changes to acceptance, routing admission, exclusions, clock boundaries, or the meaning of the boot block require Gene’s explicit ruling. The mayor schedules and lands; the mayor does not silently move the bar.
- Publish an old/new verdict matrix and reason for every changed verdict. Corrections create a new instrument epoch. Historical results remain attached to their original instrument; regrades are append-only and cannot recover observations that were never captured. Timing claims across a changed instrument require fresh matched controls.

The steward’s checker is fallible too. Anchor it with literal, independently inspectable outcomes—wrong workspace, worker exit 17, stale final candidate, an intended assertion that really fails—and retained raw events. Add bounded adversarial sampling and Gene’s periodic review. Do not commission an infinite tower of graders. A credible gate defect that could falsely authorize release invokes Andon and suspends the affected authority while repair continues.

**5. Goodhart: protect the denominator before the dashboard.**

Every attractive scalar here can be gamed. Apparatus share falls if one shell call hides twenty administrative steps, or if useful actions are artificially split. Refusal rate falls if calls are avoided or refusals relabelled. Cold-gate counts improve if failures vanish. Red-first compliance can use an irrelevant failing assertion. Oracle counts fall when the baseline or grader loosens. Wall improves by prewarming off-clock, excluding setup, shifting proof or review to somebody else, or selecting easy tasks. Daily landings reward churn; friction closure rewards deleting tickets; zero operator intervention rewards not asking for necessary help. Even fresh holdouts eventually become familiar.

Keep those diagnostics, with frozen definitions and disaggregated coverage. Use raw event sampling to audit action classifications, independent literal acceptance for correctness, and new natural tasks for transfer. Do not punish a seat for reporting friction or pulling the cord. Otherwise the loop learns silence.

There is no literally un-gameable productivity number under a subject that controls task choice, logs, oracle, and reporting. **The one meter I would protect is request-to-independently-accepted completion for a prospectively assigned task set, with every assignment retained.** The independent recorder assigns task IDs before the worker sees the task, records wall from assignment through required acceptance, and separately reports the final landing tail and Gene’s intervention time. Pending tasks continue aging; refusals, timeouts, abandonment, and unknown outcomes stay in the denominator. Do not summarize only successful durations. Publish completed/assigned counts and the latency distribution with censored outcomes visible.

Freeze intent and acceptance before optimization; the candidate cannot select its own cohort, edit the recorder, or erase unsuccessful assignments. Externally retained events and an independent acceptor protect this boundary. Matched native controls establish comparative claims; natural assigned work tests whether those claims matter in practice. Gene periodically inspects one ordinary accepted result and one failure/pending item against the original request. This cannot eliminate collusion or mistaken acceptance, but it makes the easiest cheating—changing what counts as work or dropping the hard work—visible. The outcome must remain useful when nobody is looking at the score.
